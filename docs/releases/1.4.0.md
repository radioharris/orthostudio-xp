# OrthoStudio XP 1.4.0

The clouds of a built tile can be replaced by another source's clear ground, and settings can be
kept as profiles. How to use them, step by step:
[the manual](https://github.com/radioharris/orthostudio-xp/blob/main/docs/manual.md).

## What changed

**New**
- *Clouds…* in the Library finds the clouds of a built tile and replaces each with the ground of another source that was clear there (Esri, EOX, a national source), the one you choose; the map shows the result before you apply it.
- A cloud the search missed can be outlined by hand on the map.
- The tool that finds the clouds (about 110 MB, OpenCV included) is not in the installer: it is downloaded once, when you ask, and runs on your computer.
- *Settings profiles*: Settings can be kept under a name (*Save as a new profile…*) and chosen for each build in step 1 of the Plan, which says when its source or level differs from the profile's.

**Ground** (build a tile again to get these)
- A tile whose *Coast fade precision* (Settings, for experts) is finer than its detail level keeps its coast fade: a ZL15 tile with the precision at 16 showed sharp shores.

**Interface**
- Every button, list and field has the same height, and the Plan's texts are a third shorter.
- The Plan's download estimate counts the images by the size they really have on this computer.
- The app left open no longer closes by itself after a while with the screen asleep.
- *Add*, pressed after typing tile names, no longer says the coordinates are invalid.

## Install

Download the installer of your system below, then follow *Getting started* in the README.
Installed over an older version, it keeps your tiles and settings: on macOS, quit OrthoStudio XP
first (*Quit* in the page, or Cmd+Q), then replace the app; on Windows, the installer stops a
running OrthoStudio XP by itself.

| System | File |
|---|---|
| macOS 14 or later, Apple Silicon | `OrthoStudio-XP-1.4.0-macos-arm64.dmg` |
| macOS 15 or later, Intel | `OrthoStudio-XP-1.4.0-macos-x86_64.dmg` |
| Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit | `OrthoStudio-XP-1.4.0-windows-x64-setup.exe` |
| Linux, 64-bit | `OrthoStudio-XP-1.4.0-linux-x86_64.tar.gz` |

The installers are **not signed**, so macOS and Windows warn the first time you open the app, and
a browser may refuse to download them; the README says how to open them anyway.

## Good to know

- X-Plane reads new scenery when it starts: add or remove tiles while it is closed.
- The data folder's disk must be formatted APFS, Mac OS Extended, NTFS or ext4 (not exFAT, FAT32
  or a network folder).
- The imagery and the map data come from public services whose terms apply to you: the tiles you
  build are for your own use.

## Known limitations

- **The cloud detector can take something pale for a cloud**, such as light, muddy water near a
  coast: look at a cloud on the map before you tick it.
- **Aerodromes by the sea.** Where a dried aerodrome meets the sea, its edge can stand a few metres
  above the water (6 m at Avranches, LFRW).
- **Unplugging the data folder's disk** while OrthoStudio XP reads it can close the app: plug the
  disk back and open it again, nothing is lost.

## Reporting a problem

Open an issue in this repository
([New issue](https://github.com/radioharris/orthostudio-xp/issues/new/choose)) and choose
*A problem*. Its form asks for the version, your system, what you did and what you saw, the tile
(`+46+006`), a screenshot, and the log `serve.log` (`~/Library/Logs/OrthoStudio XP` on macOS,
`%LOCALAPPDATA%\OrthoStudio XP\Logs` on Windows, `~/.orthostudio/log` on Linux).
An idea goes to the [Ideas](https://github.com/radioharris/orthostudio-xp/discussions/categories/ideas)
of the Discussions, where others can vote for it, and a question to
[Q&A](https://github.com/radioharris/orthostudio-xp/discussions/categories/q-a).
Without a GitHub account, the comments on OrthoStudio XP's page on X-Plane.Org are read too.
