# OrthoStudio XP 0.1.21 (beta)

OrthoStudio XP Live: aerial imagery under your aircraft wherever you fly, downloaded while you fly,
as AutoOrtho does, with no tiles to build first.

## What changed

- At its first start, the app asks what you want to do: fly now, with Live, or build your tiles, as
  before. It remembers the answer; each window has a button to the other.
- Live downloads the imagery ahead of the aircraft and writes the textures to the disk as ordinary
  files: X-Plane never waits for it in flight, so there is nothing to freeze on.
- At a flight's start, X-Plane waits on its loading screen until the ortho around the aircraft is
  built, as with AutoOrtho: a few seconds where you have flown before, a minute or two the first
  time in a new area. A small plugin does it, which Live puts into X-Plane while it runs and takes
  out when it stops.
- Live lays its photos on AutoOrtho's mesh, which it installs by region from its window (Europe,
  North America...), or uses AutoOrtho's regions where they are installed already. Quit AutoOrtho
  before you start Live: they serve the same scenery.
- Your own tiles come first: where OrthoStudio XP built a tile, X-Plane shows it, and Live
  downloads nothing there.
- With your SimBrief name in its settings, Live follows your flight plan, and can build the ortho
  round your departure before you start X-Plane.
- No zoom by flight level, prefetch or waiting settings: X-Plane says how sharp it wants each
  texture, and Live builds exactly that, before X-Plane asks. Live's guide has the table of every
  AutoOrtho setting and what replaces it:
  [docs/live.md](https://github.com/radioharris/orthostudio-xp/blob/main/docs/live.md#why-there-are-no-zoom-prefetch-or-waiting-settings).
- Live is on in this beta. To switch it off, put `enabled = false` in `live.toml`, in
  OrthoStudio XP's own folder (`~/.orthostudio` on macOS and Linux, `%USERPROFILE%\.orthostudio`
  on Windows).

Live has flown with X-Plane 12 on a Mac and on a Windows PC, not yet on Linux. On a computer with
16 GB of memory, keep its default zoom (16, and 18 near airports): the sharper ones need 32 GB.

## Install

Download the installer of your system below, then follow *Getting started* in the README.
Installed over an older version, it keeps your tiles and settings: on macOS, quit OrthoStudio XP
first (*Quit* in the page, or Cmd+Q), then replace the app; on Windows, the installer stops a
running OrthoStudio XP by itself, and Live with it (if X-Plane is flying with Live, it asks first).

| System | File |
|---|---|
| macOS 14 or later, Apple Silicon | `OrthoStudio-XP-0.1.21-macos-arm64.dmg` |
| macOS 15 or later, Intel | `OrthoStudio-XP-0.1.21-macos-x86_64.dmg` |
| Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit | `OrthoStudio-XP-0.1.21-windows-x64-setup.exe` |
| Linux, 64-bit (experimental) | `OrthoStudio-XP-0.1.21-linux-x86_64.tar.gz` |

The installers are **not signed** yet, so macOS and Windows warn the first time you open the app,
and a browser may refuse to download them; the README says how to open them anyway. The known
limitations of 0.1.0 still apply:
[docs/releases/0.1.0.md](https://github.com/radioharris/orthostudio-xp/blob/main/docs/releases/0.1.0.md).

## Reporting a problem

Open an issue in this repository
([New issue](https://github.com/radioharris/orthostudio-xp/issues/new/choose)). Its form asks for
the version, your system, what you did and what you saw, the tile (`+46+006`), a screenshot, and
the log `serve.log` (`~/Library/Logs/OrthoStudio XP` on macOS,
`%LOCALAPPDATA%\OrthoStudio XP\Logs` on Windows, `~/.orthostudio/log` on Linux). For Live, add
`LogLive.txt`, at the top of your X-Plane folder beside X-Plane's own `Log.txt`, and that `Log.txt`.
Without a GitHub account, the comments on OrthoStudio XP's page on X-Plane.Org are read too.
