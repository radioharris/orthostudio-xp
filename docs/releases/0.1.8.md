# OrthoStudio XP 0.1.8 (beta)

OrthoStudio XP opens in a window of its own instead of a tab in your browser. A relief for South
America takes the treetops out of the Amazon. And a page that says what is wrong at the top,
rather than going grey or hiding it in a fold at the foot.

## What changed

- **A window of its own.** Opening the app used to open a tab in your browser: the icon left the
  Dock a moment later, a second launch opened a second tab, and the two tabs did not follow each
  other, so what you did in one did not show in the other. OrthoStudio XP now opens a window,
  drawn by the web view your system already carries (WKWebView on macOS, WebView2 on Windows).
  One window, one icon that stays, and a click that brings it back. Nothing was moved into it: it
  is the same page, served by the same engine, so a browser still opens it if you prefer.
- **Closing means what it means on your system.** On macOS, closing the window puts it away and
  the app stays in the Dock, as a Mac app does: a build goes on, a click on the icon brings the
  window back, and so does the new *Window* menu. On Windows, the cross closes the app, as it
  should there. Quitting for real, by any road (*Quit* in the page, Cmd+Q, the Dock menu, the
  cross on Windows), stops the engine, and **asks first when a build is running**, in your own
  language. Answer yes and nothing is thrown away: what was already built is kept, and building
  the tile again picks up where it stopped.
- **Linux keeps the browser, and says so.** The libraries a window needs there are built by each
  distribution for its own Python, and OrthoStudio XP carries its own: installing them would be
  advice that leads nowhere, measured in a container rather than assumed. So Linux works exactly
  as it did in 0.1.7, and the page says, once and at the top, that this machine opens the browser.
- **The Windows installer asks and tells.** It offers the **WebView2 Runtime** to the machines
  without it, as a task you can turn down (turned down, the app opens the browser). It lets you
  choose where to install, stops a running OrthoStudio XP by itself, and its uninstaller removes
  the program and nothing else: it names where your settings and your tiles stay, and warns that
  the tiles installed into X-Plane are links into that folder, not copies.
- **The relief of South America (ANADEM).** Copernicus sees the treetops: over the Amazon it puts
  the ground about 17 m too high, measured here on 262 144 points. ANADEM, built by the Federal
  University of Rio Grande do Sul and Brazil's water agency, takes that vegetation out. Choose *The
  relief of South America (ANADEM), Copernicus elsewhere* and it is read for the squares it covers,
  Copernicus for the rest, as Canada's lidar already works. Nothing to download by hand: their
  files are 2 GB per zone, and OrthoStudio XP reads the 26 to 46 MB of a square out of one, in
  about ten seconds.
- **A setting this version cannot read no longer stops everything.** One value it did not know
  refused the whole of `config.toml`, and every screen of the page then stayed empty, with the
  reason in one line at the foot that nobody reads. It happens when you come back to an older
  version, or edit the file by hand. Such a value is now left out, its default is used, and the
  page says which one, at the top, with a button to Settings. Your file is left as it is until you
  save.
- **What stops a build is said at the top.** A check that failed, Triangle4XP missing for
  instance, makes every build impossible and used to show as a red pill at the foot of the page,
  behind a fold. What stops you now belongs where you are, with a button that opens the checks.
- **An engine that answers with an error says so at the top**, with a *Reload the page* button,
  instead of leaving the fields greyed out and silent (reported on flusi.info by a user who had
  nothing to go on).
- **The map says when this machine has no airports.** Ticking *Airports* and seeing nothing left
  you to wonder why. When X-Plane's own `apt.dat` cannot be read, the legend says so rather than
  keeping an empty box, and the build goes on without it.
- **The overlay question answers itself.** It has always said "Using simHeaven X-World? Choose
  None from OrthoStudio XP", and nothing checked: a user had X-World installed, kept ours as well,
  and had the roads, forests and buildings drawn twice. When such a pack is in your Custom Scenery,
  *None* is now the recommended answer, both answers say why, and on a first run it is the answer
  chosen.
- **The map is no longer cut at the foot of the page.** It was sized without counting the status
  bar below it, so its last 29 pixels sat behind that bar and the bottom of the legend was lost.
  True since 0.1.7 and in a browser too, where a window tall enough hid it. The final report of a
  build folds into one column instead of pushing the page wider than the window.
- **Every request says who is asking** (a `User-Agent` at last): services that carry us for
  nothing could not tell who was knocking, and a server may refuse an anonymous request outright,
  which is how it was found.

## Install

Download the installer of your system below, then follow *Getting started* in the README.
Installed over an older version, it keeps your tiles and settings: on macOS, quit OrthoStudio XP
first (*Quit* in the page, or Cmd+Q), then replace the app; on Windows, the installer stops a
running OrthoStudio XP by itself.

| System | File |
|---|---|
| macOS 14 or later, Apple Silicon | `OrthoStudio-XP-0.1.8-macos-arm64.dmg` |
| macOS 15 or later, Intel | `OrthoStudio-XP-0.1.8-macos-x86_64.dmg` |
| Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit | `OrthoStudio-XP-0.1.8-windows-x64-setup.exe` |
| Linux, 64-bit (experimental) | `OrthoStudio-XP-0.1.8-linux-x86_64.tar.gz` |

The installers are **not signed** yet, so macOS and Windows warn the first time you open the app,
and a browser may refuse to download them; the README says how to open them anyway. The known
limitations of 0.1.0 still apply:
[docs/releases/0.1.0.md](https://github.com/radioharris/orthostudio-xp/blob/main/docs/releases/0.1.0.md).

## Reporting a problem

Open an issue in this repository
([New issue](https://github.com/radioharris/orthostudio-xp/issues/new/choose)). Its form asks for
the version, your system, what you did and what you saw, the tile (`+46+006`), a screenshot, and
the log `serve.log` (`~/Library/Logs/OrthoStudio XP` on macOS,
`%LOCALAPPDATA%\OrthoStudio XP\Logs` on Windows, `~/.local/state/OrthoStudio XP/log` on Linux).
Without a GitHub account, the comments on OrthoStudio XP's page on X-Plane.Org are read too.
