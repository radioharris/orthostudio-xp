# OrthoStudio XP 0.1.7 (beta)

Three fixes. Two of them change what you see out of the window, and one of those had been quietly
throwing away work you had done yourself.

## What changed

- **Your own elevation files really reach the scenery.** Named as a folder, or as Canada's lidar
  laid over Copernicus, they were found, read, and then dropped: the relief stage handed the next
  stage a file that held the source underneath alone, so the tile was built without them. Measured
  on a tile of Geneva built with a lidar folder of one's own: Lake Geneva, whose level is 372.0 m,
  came out at 368.0; it now comes out at 372.00, and the mesh of the finished tile follows the
  lidar to within a metre or two instead of the radar to within ten. **If you build with a folder
  of your own, or with Canada's relief, the tiles you built before are worth building again**;
  their key has changed, so they will be. Nothing else is rebuilt.
- **The finest relief wins, both ways.** Where your file is sharper than the relief you chose, the
  whole tile is now raised to your grid rather than read at the coarser step. Where the relief you
  chose is sharper than your file, as the United States relief at a third of an arc-second is
  against a file of one second, the relief answers and the report names the file it left aside. At
  equal steps your file keeps the square, which is what it is for.
- **The painted line around every airport is gone.** X-Plane 12 softens the edge of the airport
  grass into the terrain around it with a 2D line, and keeps it with the ground rather than with
  the roads and buildings. Every tool that rebuilds the ground from photographs and copies the rest
  into an overlay copied that line too, and over a photograph it has nothing to soften: it drew an
  outline around every airfield, sand-coloured in the desert, matching no boundary anyone could
  edit. Reported for another builder on the X-Plane.Org forum (topic 349619) and true here as well:
  fifteen of them in one European tile, one per airfield. They are now left behind. Build a tile
  again and its overlay loses them, in a few seconds and without downloading anything.
- **The Works line names the relief it uses.** Choosing the United States relief while a folder of
  your own was named read *own relief file*; it now reads *USGS relief + your own files*.

Thank you to the users of the X-Plane.Org page who reported the last two, and to the one who asked
for a whole folder of his own in the first place.

## Install

Download the installer of your system below, then follow *Getting started* in the README.
Installed over an older version, it keeps your tiles and settings: on macOS, quit OrthoStudio XP
first (*Quit*, at the top right of its page), then replace the app; on Windows, the installer stops
a running OrthoStudio XP by itself.

| System | File |
|---|---|
| macOS 14 or later, Apple Silicon | `OrthoStudio-XP-0.1.7-macos-arm64.dmg` |
| macOS 15 or later, Intel | `OrthoStudio-XP-0.1.7-macos-x86_64.dmg` |
| Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit | `OrthoStudio-XP-0.1.7-windows-x64-setup.exe` |
| Linux, 64-bit (experimental) | `OrthoStudio-XP-0.1.7-linux-x86_64.tar.gz` |

The installers are **not signed** yet, so macOS and Windows warn the first time you open the app;
the README says how to open it anyway. The known limitations of 0.1.0 still apply:
[docs/releases/0.1.0.md](https://github.com/radioharris/orthostudio-xp/blob/main/docs/releases/0.1.0.md).

## Reporting a problem

Open an issue in this repository
([New issue](https://github.com/radioharris/orthostudio-xp/issues/new/choose)). Its form asks for
the version, your system, what you did and what you saw, the tile (`+46+006`), a screenshot, and
the log `serve.log` (`~/Library/Logs/OrthoStudio XP` on macOS,
`%LOCALAPPDATA%\OrthoStudio XP\Logs` on Windows, `~/.local/state/OrthoStudio XP/log` on Linux).
Without a GitHub account, the comments on OrthoStudio XP's page on X-Plane.Org are read too.
