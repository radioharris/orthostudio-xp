# OrthoStudio XP 0.1.4 (beta)

Photo colours you can see on the map, elevation from the USGS and from Canada's lidar, and the
answers to what the users of the X-Plane.Org page reported after 0.1.3.

## What changed

- **The colours of the photos, square by square and zone by zone.** Aerial imagery often comes
  too bright or too colourful, and it shows most at low height. Settings asks the question once
  for everything you build; step 1 of the Plan sets the colours of the squares you chose; and a
  zone drawn inside a square can differ again in its own polygon — each level inherits the one
  above until it names its own. Three ready-made answers (as delivered, toned down a little, a
  lot) or your own brightness, contrast and colour on three sliders. It is applied when the
  textures are encoded, which is the last step that reads the downloaded images: **changing your
  mind re-encodes the tile without downloading anything**, about 20 seconds for a one-degree
  square. Left as delivered it changes nothing, costs nothing, and keeps the tiles you already
  built.
- **The map shows the result, not a thumbnail.** A square or a zone that carries its own colours
  is repainted on the map itself, with the very arithmetic the build will encode, so you judge
  the ground you fly over rather than a postage stamp. Two buttons undo a choice: *Give the
  colours back to Settings*, and *Go back to the colours already built*, which gives each square
  the colours of the tile you already have on the disk. The Library marks a tile built with
  colours other than its square now asks for.
- **The relief of the United States (USGS 3DEP).** Settings offers it beside X-Plane 12's own
  relief, Copernicus and your own file: 1/3 arc-second, about 10 m on the ground, the finest
  openly available. About 400 MB per one-degree square, kept afterwards. Outside its coverage the
  tile is **refused** rather than built flat, and the page says which sources cover the region.
- **The relief of Canada (HRDEM lidar), laid over Copernicus.** Airborne lidar, 1 to 2 m, **bare
  earth**: where Canada has been flown the shape of the terrain is measured, where Copernicus
  carries the tree canopy. Coverage is partial, so it is laid over Copernicus, which answers
  everywhere else with no hole and no step. On a test tile over Ottawa the lidar covers the whole
  square and sits **5.4 m below Copernicus on average** (median 2.6 m, a tenth of the square more
  than 15 m below): that is the forest canopy, and it is what you fly over. Up to 50 MB per fully
  covered square, and nothing at all where the lidar never flew.
- **A zone drawn before its tiles.** A zone builds nothing on its own — a build makes whole tiles
  — so one drawn where no tile is chosen now carries **Add N tile(s)**, which chooses the tiles it
  falls in.
- **Hand-made patches, the Works screen, and the small things.** The Imagery step says
  "nothing to download" when a tile is built again with everything in the cache, instead of
  counting requests that are not made. The Library says the colours of a pack built before they
  were recorded, by asking the store. The mock page (made-up data, no network) is reachable with
  `osxp serve --mock`.
- **On the command line**: `osxp build --relief usgs` and `--relief canada`, beside `xplane`,
  `copernicus` and `view`.

## Install

Download the installer of your system below, then follow *Getting started* in the README.
Installed over an older version, it keeps your tiles and settings: on macOS, quit OrthoStudio XP
first (*Quit*, at the top right of its page), then replace the app; on Windows, the installer stops
a running OrthoStudio XP by itself.

| System | File |
|---|---|
| macOS 14 or later, Apple Silicon | `OrthoStudio-XP-0.1.4-macos-arm64.dmg` |
| macOS 15 or later, Intel | `OrthoStudio-XP-0.1.4-macos-x86_64.dmg` |
| Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit | `OrthoStudio-XP-0.1.4-windows-x64-setup.exe` |
| Linux, 64-bit (experimental) | `OrthoStudio-XP-0.1.4-linux-x86_64.tar.gz` |

The installers are **not signed** yet, so macOS and Windows warn the first time you open the app;
the README says how to open it anyway. The known limitations of 0.1.0 still apply:
[docs/releases/0.1.0.md](https://github.com/radioharris/orthostudio-xp/blob/main/docs/releases/0.1.0.md).

## Reporting a problem

Open an issue in this repository
([New issue](https://github.com/radioharris/orthostudio-xp/issues/new/choose)). Its form asks for
the version, your system, what you did and what you saw, the tile (`+46+006`), a screenshot, and
the log `serve.log` (`~/Library/Logs/OrthoStudio XP` on macOS,
`%LOCALAPPDATA%\OrthoStudio XP\Logs` on Windows, `~/.local/state/OrthoStudio XP/log` on Linux).
Without a GitHub account, the comments on OrthoStudio XP's page on X-Plane.Org are read too.
